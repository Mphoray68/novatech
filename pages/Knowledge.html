    Quick answers to the issues we see most, plus the documents you need for escalations and change requests.

    Why is my Wi-Fi not working?

    Restart the device's Wi-Fi adapter, confirm you're on the correct SSID, and check whether other users on the same floor are affected — that points to a network-wide fault rather than a device issue.

    My printer won't print

    Check the printer has paper/toner and is powered on, then remove and re-add it in your device's printer settings. If the queue is stuck, restarting the print spooler usually clears it.

    I can't log in / access a system

    Confirm your username and that Caps Lock is off. If your account is locked, log an Access ticket — Level 1 can reset most standard accounts within the same business day.

    My laptop is running slowly

    Restart the device first — this clears most temporary performance issues. If it persists after a restart, log a Hardware ticket with the approximate time it started.

    I'm not receiving emails

    Check your internet connection and mailbox storage first. If the issue affects your whole department, it's likely a mail server fault — log it as High priority.

    How do I request a new asset?

    New hardware or software requests go through the Change Request Form below, not the standard ticket form — they need manager approval before implementation.

    Escalation policy

    A ticket moves up a level when it can't be safely resolved at the current one.

    Escalate when the issue: cannot be safely resolved at the current level; requires specialist access; affects multiple users; involves infrastructure or vendor support; or has high business impact.

    Flow: User → Ticket Logging → Level 1 Diagnosis → Resolved? Yes: Close · No: Level 2 → Resolved? Yes: Close · No: Level 3 / Specialist.

    How tickets relate to network infrastructure

    Most Network-category tickets trace back to one of three layers, which is why the affected asset field matters so much when you log a ticket.

    Routers

    Handle traffic between Nova Tech's internal network and the internet. A router fault usually shows up as a site-wide outage rather than a single user's issue.

    Switches

    Connect devices within a floor or department. A faulty switch typically affects everyone plugged into it, which is why "one user or multiple?" is the first diagnostic question.

    LANs

    The local network segment a department sits on. Recording the affected asset and department lets the technician see whether a problem is isolated or spreading across the LAN.
